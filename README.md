# Personal README
## Communication Preferences & Typical Working Hours

I generally do my best thinking and problem-solving work earlier in the day. I'm naturally more focused in the morning and tend to prefer tackling analytical or complex work before meetings and interruptions accumulate.

I appreciate direct, concise communication. If something can be said in three sentences instead of ten, I usually prefer the shorter version.

For discussions, I prefer:

- Clear expectations and desired outcomes
- Context on why something matters
- Written communication for complex topics when possible
- Fewer meetings with a clear purpose

When I have enough context, I prefer being given ownership of a problem and space to work through it independently.

## Values & Decision Making Style

### Core Values:

Ownership & Autonomy-
I do my best work when I have clear goals, ownership over outcomes, and the freedom to determine the best path forward. While I value alignment and collaboration, I appreciate being trusted to execute independently.

Continuous Improvement-
I am naturally drawn toward improving systems, processes, and skills. I enjoy finding opportunities to make work more effective, efficient, and scalable over time rather than simply maintaining the status quo.

Truth-Seeking & Intellectual Honesty-
I value understanding how things actually work. I tend to ask questions, challenge assumptions, and look for evidence before forming conclusions. This comes from a desire to make sound decisions and understand root causes rather than accept conventional wisdom at face value.

### Decision-Making Style:

I tend to think in systems rather than isolated tasks. I like understanding how individual pieces fit into the larger process, business objective, or workflow.

When making decisions, I generally prioritize:

Long-term effectiveness over short-term convenience
Simplicity over unnecessary complexity
Data and evidence over assumptions
Understanding root causes rather than treating symptoms

I often ask "why" multiple times before accepting a recommendation or process. This isn't intended as resistance—it's how I build understanding and confidence in decisions.

## Feedback

I prefer feedback that is:

- Direct
- Specific
- Actionable
- Timely

I generally don't need feedback to be heavily softened. If something isn't working, I'd rather know early so I can correct it quickly.

The most helpful feedback includes:

- What happened
- Why it matters
- What success would look like instead

I also appreciate feedback on areas for improvement more than general praise, although knowing what is working well helps me understand where to continue investing effort.

When giving feedback to others, I try to focus on ideas, processes, and outcomes rather than personalities.

## Motivations

I'm motivated by learning, growth, and mastery.

The environments where I perform best typically include:

- Meaningful autonomy
- Clear ownership
- Opportunities to learn new skills
- Challenging problems that require analytical thinking
- A balance between structure and flexibility

I enjoy understanding how systems work end-to-end and am energized when I can connect technical work to broader outcomes.

I tend to be most engaged when I can see steady progress toward a meaningful goal and when my work contributes to building something useful or improving a process.

Support that helps me succeed:

- Clear priorities
- Context around objectives
- Trust and autonomy
- Honest feedback
- Opportunities to expand my skillset

## Blind Spots & Areas I want to improve

### Balancing Analysis with Execution

I naturally enjoy understanding systems and exploring different perspectives. Occasionally this can lead me to spend more time analyzing than acting. I'm working on recognizing when additional analysis has diminishing returns and execution is the better next step.

### Communicating at the Right Level of Detail

Because I enjoy understanding the underlying mechanics of a problem, I sometimes provide more context than necessary. I'm working on tailoring communication to the audience and focusing on the information that is most relevant to the decision at hand.

### Prioritization under ambiguity

I enjoy exploring multiple possibilities and viewpoints. I'm working on becoming faster at identifying the highest-leverage path forward when information is incomplete.
